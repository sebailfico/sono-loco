Drop tones

The notification sounds, made here with the integer steps lib/tones uses: a water drop that glides up into its note, through an echo. Two drops at one pitch mean listening, three rising mean joined, three falling mean nothing heard. Tune by ear, then copy the settings into config.h and the figures into test_tones.

Voice

The three sounds

Dots are drops, placed by pitch. Fainter ones are echoes.

Tune

An echo time of 1.5 note gaps puts every echo between two drops. Off that ratio, an echo can land on a drop and smear it.

Settings

The values for config.h, and the figures test_tones checks the firmware against. 

    

  


  

    
On the board

    
    
      
  • Integer arithmetic only: a 1,024-step sine table, a phase counter, multiplies and shifts. The C3 has no FPU, and sinf() per sample is what made its beeps stutter.
    • 
      
  • The echo is not a delay buffer. Each drop is played again, later and quieter, as one more voice. A sound needs a few hundred bytes of state and no heap, which matters on a Bluetooth server.
    • 
      
  • The glass is two-operator FM, the sound of DX7 bells and polyphonic ringtones. Each echo keeps less of it, the way a tape echo loses its top end.
    • 
      
  • The same sample goes to both channels, as now. A ping-pong echo would lose half its repeats on a MAX98357A, which plays one channel.
    • 
      
  • Each sound holds loop() for as long as it plays: the length under it, plus 50 ms of silence that lets the DMA play the end out. Power-on waits for the listening sound, and a server's connect jingle holds its output for the joined one.
    • 
      
  • What plays here is what a speaker gets, apart from loudness: each node sets that with TONE_AMPLITUDE, and the peak under each sound is relative to it. The old beeps peaked at 1.00. test_tones checks the firmware against the figures in the settings, sample for sample.
